# Threat T27: Mempool partitioning via network control, ver. 1.0 (final)

Red Team, IO|Labs <br> Simulation study: June 2026; final report: 19 November
2026

## Abstract

Threat T27 describes an adversary controlling network infrastructure to give
block producers inconsistent transaction pools. This can prevent endorser blocks
(EBs) from gathering enough votes, delay transactions, and waste processing
resources.

In the recorded Linear Leios simulations, complete isolation of one region
caused a temporary certification stall. After connectivity returned, the primary
conflict-free run recovered to nearly the same block-inclusion total as its
unpartitioned baseline. A separate higher-load run also drained its backlog. The
whole-run average transaction delay in the primary comparison was higher, but
the logs do not establish persistent slowdown for new transactions after
recovery. A run with conflicting transactions also resumed endorsements.

These are simulator findings intending to exercise the protocol behavior at a
large scale resembling mainnet topology. They do not establish how the Haskell
Leios implementation behaves under the same conditions.

## Experimentation

The study examines two effects: voting work on EBs that fail to reach quorum,
and the handling of conflicting transactions in inconsistent mempools. The
conflict-free runs test the first effect; a separate conflict workload exercises
the second. Exercising that workload is distinct from proving that conflicting
transactions never reach the ledger.

The study used the Rust simulator from `leios-tools`, a pseudo-mainnet topology
of 2,685 nodes, seed 0, a sequential engine, and six simulation shards. The main
variant was `linear-with-tx-references`, where EBs reference transaction
contents that voters must obtain separately.

The adversarial action was a bidirectional network partition: 1,052 European
nodes were isolated from the other 1,633 nodes, cutting 84,746 directed edges in
the simulated network. All traffic across that boundary was blocked. The study
therefore tests complete network isolation, a stronger disruption than
selectively blocking transaction propagation. The adversary only changed network
connectivity. Transaction generation, including the conflicts in the additional
experiment, was a background-workload property, not a separate adversarial
action.

The primary comparison used a nominal load of 120 kilobytes per second,
1,500-byte transactions, and 1,500 one-second slots. Both runs generated 115,200
transactions. The partition lasted from simulated time 300 to 700 seconds. In
the logs, its events follow the announcements for slots 299 and 699. The study
describes neither side as holding the configured 75% voting stake threshold; the
observed endorsement stall is consistent with that setup.

The 120-kilobyte-per-second load was chosen below the default 200 to provide a
baseline whose backlog could drain. The historical analysis identifies the rate
of certificate inclusion in ranking blocks as a capacity constraint and treats
150 kilobytes per second as near its estimated limit. These are observations
about this configuration, not a general throughput limit for Leios.

## Normal behaviour

Without a partition, the simulator reported 110,280 transaction references
through Leios endorsements and 2,280 direct inclusions in Praos blocks. Their
sum was 97.71% of offered transactions. Mean transaction-to-block time was 84.1
seconds, and final mean pending-transaction age was 16.5 seconds.

The baseline also experienced backlog fluctuations and unsuccessful EBs: 6 of 64
EBs failed to reach the vote threshold. Partition effects must therefore be
assessed against this baseline, rather than against an assumption that every EB
succeeds immediately.

## Comparison of complete runs

The table compares final results from two 1,500-second runs: one without a
partition, and one with isolation during seconds 300–700.

| Measurement                                                       | Run without partition | Run with partition (300–700 s) |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------: | -----------------------------: |
| Transactions offered                                              |               115,200 |                        115,200 |
| Transaction references through Leios endorsements                 |               110,280 |                        110,072 |
| Direct transaction inclusions in Praos blocks                     |                 2,280 |                          2,340 |
| Block-inclusion proxy / offered transactions                      |                97.71% |                         97.58% |
| EBs below the vote threshold                                      |                6 / 64 |                        17 / 64 |
| EBs expired before an EB from their stage reached a ranking block |               31 / 64 |                        38 / 64 |
| WrongEB validation failures                                       |                 5,396 |                          1,800 |
| Redundant endorsement references                                  |            0 (0.000%) |                    18 (0.016%) |
| Whole-run mean transaction-to-block time                          |          84.1 seconds |                  214.6 seconds |
| Final mean pending-transaction age                                |          16.5 seconds |                   68.5 seconds |

Legend: s = seconds; EB = endorser block. WrongEB = the simulator's reason for
rejecting a vote attempt associated with an unexpected EB. The block-inclusion
proxy is the sum of endorsement references and direct Praos inclusions divided
by transactions offered; it is not an independently verified count of unique,
finalized ledger transactions. Redundant references count repeated references to
a transaction, not distinct transactions spending the same input. The expiry
counter is distinct from failure to reach the vote threshold.

Sources: the saved baseline log (`topology-v4-mainnet-120kb-1500sl-full`) and
partition log (`topology-v4-mainnet-120kb-1500sl-eu-isolate`; raw logs retained
internally), final protocol
statistics.

The number of blocks carrying endorsements rose from 4 at slot 300 to 7 at slot
360, then stayed at 7 through slot 720. Over slots 360–720, the baseline rose
from 5 to 12. Thus the partitioned run added no endorsed blocks in that interval
while the baseline added seven. At slot 780, after reconnection, the partitioned
counter had risen to 8. This confirms an endorsement stall after an initial
transition; the aggregate logs do not establish when each individual certificate
formed or that none formed throughout the entire partition.

Endorsed transaction references remained at 18,402 from the slot-360 report
through slot 720, then increased after reconnection. The sampled mean pending
age peaked at 427.1 seconds at slot 1080. Node 0's mempool peaked at 26,796
transactions at slot 1140 and returned to the baseline's 3,518 transactions by
slot 1380, about 680 seconds after reconnection.

The partition therefore caused substantial delay even though the final
block-inclusion proxy differed by only 0.13 percentage points. The whole-run
latency average includes transactions delayed during the partition and backlog
drain; it does not isolate transactions submitted after recovery. Final pending
age describes the transactions still waiting, not the latency of new arrivals.
The lower WrongEB count does not demonstrate improved voting: fewer
opportunities to vote during isolation can also reduce this counter.

## Additional experiments

### Higher transaction load

At 150 kilobytes per second, with isolation from seconds 300 to 500, the
simulator reported 138,340 endorsed transaction references and 2,280 direct
Praos inclusions out of 144,000 offered transactions: a 97.65% block-inclusion
proxy. Node 0's mempool declined from a sampled peak of 33,722 to 4,560 at
slot 1440. Final mean pending age was 42.4 seconds. Of 64 EBs, 13 failed to
reach the voting threshold and 36 expired before an EB from their stage reached
a ranking block. WrongEB failures numbered 4,012; redundant endorsement
references were zero.

This run (`topology-v4-mainnet-150kb-1500sl-eu-isolate`; raw log retained
internally) supports recovery at
higher load. Without a matching unpartitioned run at that load, it cannot
separate partition effects from capacity limits. In particular, the 36 expired
EBs were measured in the partitioned run; they do not establish how many would
expire at this load without a partition.

### Partial regional cut

An earlier experiment cut direct connections between Europe and North America
while leaving routes through other regions. Its
[run report](eu-na-partition-run.md) describes a temporary voting drop and
subsequent recovery, but no matching raw run log is retained here to verify its
magnitude. The remaining routes distinguish this partial cut from complete
isolation. The experiment does not establish that partial cuts are harmless or
that complete isolation is necessary for every T27 effect.

### Conflicting transactions

A further run used 120 kilobytes per second, a configured 10% probability of
generating a transaction conflicting with a recent input, and a longer partition
from seconds 300 to 900. The 10% rate is a stress setting, not a measured
mainnet conflict rate. The model reuses a single input identifier and does not
model scripts or collateral. Endorsements resumed after reconnection.

The saved log (`topology-v4-mainnet-120kb-1500sl-eu-isolate-conflict`; retained
internally) reports
102,620 of 115,200 transactions included in at least one EB, 96,560 endorsed
transaction references, 22 of 64 EBs below quorum, and final mean pending age of
539.2 seconds. At the last periodic snapshot, slot 1440, node 0 had 7,893
mempool entries and 90,844 entries in its spent-input set; redundant endorsement
references were zero. WrongEB failures totalled 1,800, matching the
conflict-free partitioned run, while 41 of 64 EBs expired before an EB from
their stage reached a ranking block.

Node 0's mempool decreased from 34,022 transactions at slot 1140 to 7,893 at
slot 1440, indicating that the backlog was shrinking. Because this run also used
a longer partition, the results cannot separate the effects of conflicting
transactions from those of partition duration.

The final log ends immediately after the 96,560-reference counter. The direct
Praos inclusion count of 2,358 and zero redundant references quoted in the
earlier analysis are available at slot 1440, not in the final summary. They
should not be combined with final counters as an exact finalization result.

Inclusion in an EB does not establish finalization. Likewise, zero repeated
transaction references and a growing spent-input set do not prove that two
different conflicting transactions never reached the ledger. The observations
are consistent with conflict rejection, but do not establish ledger safety or
that finalization reached the maximum possible after excluding conflicting
alternatives.

## Discussion

The clearest supported effect is temporary loss of certification progress and
increased transaction delay. Recovery is demonstrated for the tested
conflict-free runs; the conflict run shows resumed endorsements with a remaining
backlog. These finite runs support temporary disruption, rather than a permanent
failure to resume progress after reconnection.

The
[threat model](https://github.com/input-output-hk/ouroboros-leios/blob/main/docs/threat-model.md#transaction-based-denial-of-service)
rates T27's mitigation as limited. Transactions flow from clients towards block
producers, whereas block diffusion carries endorsed data back towards voters and
the wider network; disrupting those flows requires different network
positioning. The model expects Linear Leios to prevent conflicting transactions
from reaching permanent storage and therefore primarily incur temporary, local
resource waste. Recovery in these runs is consistent with that expectation, but
the saved counters do not independently verify ledger safety or establish that
every impact is local.

The study covers one topology and one seed. It does not establish outcomes for
repeated or sustained isolation, or selective partitions that leave both sides
productive. Complete isolation need not maximize wasted voting: a selective cut
could keep divergent groups productive, while a longer or recurring cut could
prolong the stall. The tested cuts lasted 200, 400, and 600 seconds. Variability
across seeds and topologies was not measured.

Earlier non-recovery observations were attributed to an unimplemented simulator
transaction-fetch path (`RequestEBTxs` / `EBTxs`), not a demonstrated protocol
failure. Those obsolete reports were removed; the retained runs use the
corrected path. The study did not compare its behaviour with the live node's
`LeiosFetch`.

The inline-transaction control
(`topology-v4-mainnet-120kb-1500sl-leios-variant-linear-eu-isolate`; raw log
retained internally) reached the same final inclusion counters as the reference-based variant. The
saved material does not pin an exact simulator revision. The current
`eu-isolate.yaml` specifies seconds 300–900, whereas the primary comparison used
seconds 300–700.

The related [T27a study](../t27a/tx-flooding.md) reports live Musashi
experiments using transaction flooding from unstaked sources. It investigates a
related mempool-fragmentation hypothesis through a different mechanism and does
not validate these network-partition simulations against Haskell nodes.

Detailed setup and analysis of the recorded runs are available in
[network-partition.md](network-partition.md),
[partition-150kb-eu-isolate.md](partition-150kb-eu-isolate.md),
[partition-120kb-conflict.md](partition-120kb-conflict.md), and
[eu-na-partition-run.md](eu-na-partition-run.md). The measurements and evidence
qualifications in this final report take precedence over stronger
interpretations in those earlier reports.

## Results

Complete regional isolation temporarily stalled Leios certification in the
tested simulator configuration. After reconnection, the conflict-free runs
reduced their backlog. The primary comparison approached baseline block
inclusion with a higher whole-run average transaction delay; persistent slowdown
for transactions submitted after recovery was not established.

The conflict run resumed progress but does not independently prove double-spend
safety or complete recovery. Thus the failed-quorum effect is demonstrated,
while the conflicting-transaction workload provides only indirect evidence of
safe conflict handling. These findings apply to the simulated workloads,
topology, and finite partition windows examined here.
